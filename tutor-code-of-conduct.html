Tutor Code of Conduct

These guidelines describe the professional behaviour expected from tutors associated with TutorServices while teaching students, communicating with families and representing their qualifications and services.

Quick Answer

What professional conduct does TutorServices expect?

Tutors are expected to represent their background honestly, prepare suitable lessons, arrive on time, communicate respectfully, protect student information and maintain appropriate professional boundaries. They should teach only subjects and levels they can support competently and avoid promises of guaranteed marks, ranks, fluency or admission.

Scope: This page communicates expected standards and practical guidance. It does not claim a universal monitoring, disciplinary or enforcement process beyond the review and communication practices TutorServices actually uses.

Core principles

  • Act honestly, safely and professionally.
  • Put the learner's educational needs first.
  • Maintain clear boundaries and communication.

Shared responsibility

  • Families should provide accurate requirements.
  • Students should participate and practise consistently.
  • All parties should clarify schedules and fees.
Our Commitment

Professional tutoring should support learning, safety and mutual respect

TutorServices aims to connect students with tutors whose submitted experience, subject expertise, class levels, boards, location and availability are relevant to the enquiry. A suitable introduction is only the beginning. Trust develops through consistent preparation, honest communication and responsible behaviour during every lesson.

Students deserve explanations that respect their age, current understanding and educational goals. Parents deserve timely information about schedules, progress and concerns. Tutors deserve accurate requirements, reasonable communication and a safe, respectful working environment. The strongest learning relationships recognise these responsibilities on all sides.

Professional standards matter in both home tuition and online learning. In-person lessons require clear household boundaries and appropriate arrangements for minors. Online lessons require secure communication, suitable technology and the same level of courtesy expected in a physical learning space.

This code should be read with the transparent tutor verification process. Profile review helps assess relevance, but it cannot guarantee conduct or academic results. Families and tutors should discuss expectations before regular classes and raise concerns promptly through appropriate channels.

Tutor Responsibilities

Standards for responsible and effective teaching

These expectations help tutors create a dependable learning relationship without overstating what any lesson or programme can guarantee.

01

Represent qualifications honestly

Provide accurate qualifications, experience, subjects, boards, classes, locations and availability during tutor registration. Never claim certificates, results, teaching experience or institutional associations that cannot be supported. Teach only the levels and topics you can handle competently, and update the team when important profile details change.

02

Behave professionally

Use courteous language, suitable clothing and a calm manner. Avoid discriminatory, humiliating, threatening or aggressive behaviour. Do not use lessons for unrelated selling, political persuasion, personal disputes or inappropriate conversations. A tutor's authority should support learning, never intimidate the student or family.

03

Be punctual and dependable

Arrive or join on time, teach for the agreed duration and communicate early when an unavoidable delay or cancellation occurs. Repeated last-minute changes disrupt learning and family schedules. Agree on reasonable notice, rescheduling and missed-session expectations before regular tuition begins rather than negotiating them after a disagreement.

04

Prepare every lesson

Review the learner's class, board, syllabus and current topic. Bring suitable explanations, examples and practice rather than relying only on improvised homework completion. A prepared lesson should connect new material with prior knowledge, check understanding and leave the student with a clear next step.

05

Teach with patience and respect

Students learn at different speeds. Explain errors without ridicule, invite questions and adapt examples where possible. Do not compare a learner negatively with siblings or classmates. Constructive challenge is valuable, but pressure should remain age-appropriate and should not create fear around asking questions or attempting difficult work.

06

Maintain appropriate boundaries

Keep communication and interaction connected to the educational arrangement. For minors, include parents or guardians in important scheduling and progress discussions. Avoid private meetings in unsuitable settings, inappropriate physical contact, personal relationships, requests for unnecessary private information or communication that bypasses agreed family boundaries.

07

Protect confidentiality

Use student names, contact details, addresses, academic records, photographs and family information only for the relevant tuition arrangement. Do not share them publicly, include them in promotional material or forward them to another person without appropriate permission. Store any notes or documents carefully and remove unnecessary copies when no longer needed.

08

Communicate progress accurately

Share specific observations about covered topics, recurring errors, attendance, practice and next goals. Do not exaggerate improvement or conceal persistent difficulties. Feedback should be factual and useful. If the student's needs fall outside the tutor's expertise, say so early rather than continuing with unsuitable instruction.

09

Set transparent fees and terms

State the preferred fee structure accurately, including Preferred fee (Per hour in Rupees) when hourly tuition is selected. Before regular lessons, confirm the final fee, duration, frequency, payment timing, travel considerations, cancellation expectations and whether a demo is free or paid. Avoid unexpected charges or pressure for unclear advance payments.

10

Avoid unrealistic guarantees

Do not guarantee marks, ranks, admissions, examination selection, fluency or a particular timeline of improvement. Explain what teaching can reasonably provide: structured guidance, concept clarification, practice, feedback and study planning. Outcomes also depend on attendance, prior knowledge, independent work, health and examination conditions.

Online Teaching Expectations

Create a secure, focused and professional digital classroom

Online convenience does not reduce the tutor's responsibility for preparation, boundaries or respectful communication.

Reliable setup

Use stable internet, working audio, a charged device and a backup communication plan. Test unfamiliar teaching tools before the lesson.

Professional environment

Teach from a quiet, suitably lit setting with an appropriate background. Reduce interruptions and keep unrelated people out of the session.

Secure communication

Use agreed platforms and meeting links. Do not record, photograph or distribute a session without appropriate permission from the learner or guardian.

Active participation

Use questions, worked examples and checks for understanding. Online teaching should not become a continuous lecture with little student involvement.

Practical online lesson checklist

  1. Confirm the platform, time and topic before the session.
  2. Keep files, examples and exercises ready to share.
  3. Check that the student can hear, see and participate.
  4. Protect meeting links, recordings and personal information.
  5. End with a brief summary and realistic next action.
Student & Parent Expectations

Professional tuition works through mutual cooperation

The code primarily guides tutors, but a productive arrangement also depends on accurate information, respectful communication and learner participation.

Accurate requirements

Families should share the correct class, board, subjects, locality, schedule, learning mode and goals. Important learning needs should be discussed without expecting a tutor to diagnose conditions or provide support outside their competence.

Suitable learning environment

Home learners need a practical study space and appropriate guardian involvement for minors. Online learners need a working device, stable connection and minimal distractions. Tutors should not be expected to teach effectively in unsafe or persistently disruptive conditions.

Respectful scheduling

Families and tutors should follow agreed times and provide reasonable notice when plans change. Both sides should maintain professional communication and avoid repeated calls or messages at inappropriate hours unless an urgent arrangement has been agreed.

Consistent participation

Students should attend, ask questions, complete appropriate practice and explain when they do not understand. A tutor can guide and monitor learning, but cannot replace the student's own effort or guarantee results without consistent participation.

If expectations become unclear, review the TutorServices FAQs or contact TutorServices with the specific concern. Relevant feedback may be reviewed to understand the issue and improve future matching, but this page does not claim a universal formal dispute or enforcement procedure.

Continuous Improvement

Strong tutors keep learning as well as teaching

Subject knowledge, curricula and student needs change. Tutors should periodically review current textbooks, board requirements, assessment patterns and reliable academic resources. Senior-level tutors should practise advanced problems rather than depending only on earlier qualifications.

Teaching skill also deserves attention. Clear explanations, diagnostic questions, examples, spaced revision and constructive feedback can be improved through reflection. After a lesson, tutors can ask what the student understood, where confusion remained and whether the planned activity produced useful evidence of learning.

Constructive feedback should be considered professionally. Not every concern means the tutor lacks knowledge; sometimes the pace, examples, communication or schedule is simply a poor fit. Listening without defensiveness allows tutors to adjust where reasonable and identify situations where another tutor may better serve the learner.

A simple improvement cycle

  1. Review subject and syllabus updates.
  2. Set one clear lesson objective.
  3. Check understanding during the lesson.
  4. Record recurring errors and progress.
  5. Use feedback to adjust the next plan.
